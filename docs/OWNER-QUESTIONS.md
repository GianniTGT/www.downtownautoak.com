# What only the owner can decide

One conversation, in this order. The first two decide whether there is a
business; the rest decide what the site says.

## The two that come first

1. **Does the insurance cover renting vehicles to the public?**
   A dealer's garage liability policy almost certainly does not. One call to the
   agent. Nothing else matters until it is answered.

2. **Do the licences cover it?**
   Alaska state business licence (AS 43.70) plus whatever the Municipality
   requires for a rental operation. Confirm the existing dealer licence covers
   this activity — it may not. Ask the DMV whether vehicles rented to the public
   need commercial registration rather than dealer plates.

## Then

3. **How many vehicles, and which?** Three is a business; one is a favour. Staying
   under 35 keeps the federal recall-grounding rule (49 U.S.C. §30120) off, and
   checking every VIN against NHTSA before it goes out is still right practice —
   the site says so, and the checklist tracks it.

4. **Which gravel roads are allowed?** The whole proposition, and a real risk
   decision: a recovery from the Dalton costs thousands. Whatever is decided, the
   website and the rental agreement must use the same words. → *Rentals → Setup →
   Roads*, and the same sentence into the agreement.

5. **Airport pick-up, or lot only?** An ANC concession costs a percentage of
   revenue and a contract; a shuttle from the lot costs a driver's time.
   Off-airport is the sane start.

6. **Rates by season, and the deposit.** → *Setup → Seasons*, and each vehicle's
   daily / weekly / monthly rate.

7. **Rent-to-own: how much of the rental credits against the purchase?**
   → *Setup → Policies → Rent-to-own credit*. It appears on every vehicle page.

8. **Pets, smoking, additional drivers, minimum age.** → *Setup → Policies*.

9. **One-way to Fairbanks, Seward, Homer, Whittier — offered or not, and at what
   fee?** "Ask us" is an acceptable answer. Silence is not.

10. **Who answers the requests, and how fast?** A promise on the site is a
    promise. → *Setup → Policies → Response promise*.

## Facts to look up rather than remember

Nothing below is written into the code, and the checklist will keep saying "not
done" until somebody has verified it and typed the date they did:

- **The vehicle rental tax rates** — AS 43.52 (state) and AMC 12.50 (Anchorage),
  from the Alaska Department of Revenue, with the filing schedule. Do not trust
  any figure written from memory, including one in the build brief.
- **The studded-tire window** for north of 60°N, from the current statute.
- **The damage waiver.** Selling a CDW/LDW is regulated as an insurance-adjacent
  product in several states. Until an attorney answers that for Alaska, the site
  does not sell one: it quotes the rate and handles protection at the counter.
- **The rental agreement itself** is the attorney's, and must settle gravel
  permission, mileage, who may drive, the deposit and the hold, fuel, damage,
  ferry and Canada permissions, and recovery costs from a road the vehicle was
  not permitted on.

## The rule that earns the most trust

The site collects a name, a telephone number, an email, dates, a vehicle and
extras. **Never** a Social Security number, a date of birth, a driver's licence
number, or bank or card details. The licence is examined in person, at the
counter, where it can be looked at. That sentence is on the booking form, and the
software refuses to store those fields if anything ever tries.
